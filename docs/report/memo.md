# What does a late delivery cost?

A late delivery costs the platform about **R$2 [US$0.62] in lost commission** (range R$0.60–R$4.42 [US$0.18–US$1.33]), but almost all of this rests on outside estimates of how lower ratings cut sellers’ later sales. The directly measured repeat-purchase loss is only R$0.06 [US$0.02]; a clear change in repeat buying was not detected.

## Evidence and limits

Estimated harm: about **52 more unhappy reviews per 100 late orders**. Matching similar orders agrees. On the same scale, a comparison near the promised date gives 12, but cannot measure the promise’s effect reliably because orders either side differ. Excluding disruption periods lowers the headline to 49. Orders were not assigned at random; unmeasured differences may explain some harm.

## What to measure next

The biggest unknown is how long a bad review hurts later sales. At assumed lifetimes of 30, 90 and 180 days, the cost is R$0.99 [US$0.30], R$2.07 [US$0.62] and R$3.16 [US$0.95], respectively. Measure that duration in Olist’s own channel sales; check whether buyers see seller ratings.

**Support costs are separate, outside this range.** If each late delivery triggers 1 support contact (unmeasured), add about R$6 [US$1.90]. Measure contacts from Olist’s support records next.

**Illustrative annual loss at this dataset’s volume:** about 3,911 late deliveries, costing R$8,078 [US$2,425] a year. This may not be Olist’s full volume.

## Recommendation

Fund faster logistics only where avoiding a late delivery costs less than about **R$2 [US$0.62]**, using the range above. **Make promised dates accurate before buying speed:** a broken promise hurts reviews as much as about 105 extra delivery days—a rough comparison, not a tested trade-off. **Test longer promised dates first**, before rolling them out: the data do not show whether longer promises deter purchases.
